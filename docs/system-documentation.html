System Overview & Data Flow

The FINETHY Fact-Finder is an automated pre-meeting intelligence system for Certified Financial Planners. A client fills out a multi-step intake form covering demographics, income, assets, liabilities, goals, insurance, and risk tolerance. The system then runs a comprehensive financial health analysis and generates a PDF report for the advisor — before the first meeting even begins.

Technology Stack

  • Frontend: Next.js 14, React, Tailwind CSS
  • Backend: Next.js API Routes (Server Actions)
  • Database: SQLite via Prisma ORM
  • PDF Engine: @react-pdf/renderer
  • Charts: SVG Path-based pie charts
  • Fonts: Noto Sans (embedded Base64 TTF)
Key Principle: Every number shown in the report is derived, not stored. The database holds only raw client inputs. All ratios, scores, flags, and recommendations are computed fresh each time the report is generated.

Financial Health Metrics

The engine evaluates 10+ financial health metrics, each classified into a RED YELLOW GREEN traffic light status. The advisor sees at a glance which areas need urgent attention.

2.1 Emergency Fund Ratio

Measures whether the client has enough liquid assets to cover expenses during an emergency.

Formula Emergency Fund Ratio = Liquid Assets / Monthly Expenses
Liquid Assets Breakdown Liquid Assets = Bank Savings & FD + Cash at Hand + MF Debt Component
MF Debt = MF Value × (1 - Equity% / 100)
StatusThresholdInterpretation
RED< 3 monthsCritically low — one job loss or medical event could be devastating
YELLOW3 - 6 monthsMarginal — building up but not yet comfortable
GREEN≥ 6 monthsWell-cushioned for emergencies

2.2 Savings Rate

Formula Savings Rate = (Monthly Income - Expenses - EMIs) / Monthly Income × 100
StatusThresholdInterpretation
RED< 10%Unsustainable — not building any wealth
YELLOW10% - 20%Below ideal, room for improvement
GREEN≥ 20%Healthy savings discipline

2.3 EMI-to-Income Ratio

Formula EMI Ratio = Total Monthly EMIs / Monthly Income × 100
StatusThresholdInterpretation
RED> 50%Severely over-leveraged
YELLOW40% - 50%High but manageable with discipline
GREEN< 40%Comfortable debt servicing capacity

2.4 Equity Exposure vs. Age

The classic "100 minus age" rule applied to investible (non-real-estate) assets.

Formula Investible Assets = Liquid + Equity + Retirement Accounts + Gold
Equity Ratio = Equity Assets / Investible Assets × 100
Ideal Equity = max(0, 100 - Age)
Equity Assets Breakdown Equity Assets = Stocks + Other Assets + MF Equity Component
MF Equity = MF Value × (Equity% / 100)   // defaults to 100% if not specified
StatusThresholdInterpretation
REDDeviation > 25pts from idealWay too aggressive or too conservative for age
YELLOWDeviation 15-25ptsSlight misalignment
GREENDeviation < 15ptsWell-aligned with age-based benchmark

Risk Profiling Engine

The risk profile combines two independent scores into a final composite that guides asset allocation recommendations.

3.1 Tolerance Score (Willingness)

Derived from 14 psychometric questions (Q1-Q14), each scored 1-5. Measures the client's emotional comfort with volatility.

Formula Raw Score = Σ Q1..Q14
Tolerance = ((Raw - 14) / 56) × 100

Min raw = 14 (all 1s), Max raw = 70 (all 5s). Normalised to 0-100.

3.2 Capacity Score (Ability)

Derived from financial facts — not opinions. Measures whether the client can actually afford to take risk.

Method Multiple constraints evaluated.
Each assigns a sub-score (0-100).
Capacity = min(all sub-scores)

The weakest link determines capacity — one severe constraint caps the whole score.

Capacity Constraint Factors

FactorConditionSub-ScoreConstraint Label
Emergency Fund< 3 months expenses25"No emergency cushion"
Emergency Fund3-6 months55"Thin emergency cushion"
Emergency Fund≥ 6 months85"Solid emergency fund"
Savings Rate< 10%30"Very low savings rate"
Savings Rate10-20%55"Below-average savings rate"
Savings Rate≥ 20%85"Healthy savings rate"
Age≥ 5535"Less than 10 years to retirement"
Age45-5455"Approaching retirement"
Age< 4585"Long investment horizon"
EMI Ratio> 50%20"Very high EMI burden"
Dependents> 340"High number of dependents"

3.3 Final Score & Profile

Final Score Final Risk Score = min(Capacity, Tolerance)
Why min()? A client who is emotionally willing to take high risk but financially cannot afford it should NOT be put into aggressive products. Similarly, a wealthy client who is terrified of volatility should not be pushed. The lower of the two scores governs the recommendation.

3.4 Consistency Check

The engine detects contradictions in the psychometric answers:

  • Panic vs Greed: If Q1 ("sell everything") scores ≤ 2 but Q2 ("+40%/-35%") scores ≥ 4 — the client claims comfort with high volatility but would panic at the first dip.
  • Knowledge vs Behavior: If Q12 (market knowledge) scores ≥ 4 but Q14 (portfolio drawdown reaction) scores ≤ 2 — claimed expertise doesn't match emotional reactions.

3.5 Gap Insight

Logic IF Tolerance - Capacity > 15: "Willing but unable"
IF Capacity - Tolerance > 15: "Able but unwilling"
ELSE: "Aligned"

Goals & Retirement Projections

5.1 Education / General Goals

Each non-retirement goal is inflated to its future value, then a monthly SIP is back-calculated.

Future Value of Goal FV = Target Amount × (1 + inflation)years
Monthly SIP Required (at 10% p.a.) r = 0.10 / 12   // monthly rate
n = years × 12   // total months
SIP = FV × r / ((1+r)n - 1) × (1+r))

5.2 Retirement Goal

Retirement uses a two-step process: first compute the required corpus, then find the SIP needed to build it.

Step 1: Retirement Corpus (Present Value of Annuity) Annual Expense at Retirement = Current Income × 0.8 × (1+0.06)years to retire

Required Corpus = Annual Expense × (1 - (1+0.05)-20) / 0.05
Assumptions: 80% income replacement ratio, 6% general inflation, 5% real post-retirement return, 20-year drawdown period.
Step 2: Monthly SIP to Build Corpus Same SIP formula as above, with FV = Required Corpus and return = 10% p.a.

5.3 Goal Feasibility Check

The CFP sheet sums all goal SIPs and compares against the monthly surplus:

Tension Detection IF Total SIP > Monthly Surplus:
    "Retirement SIP cannot run simultaneously with Education SIPs at current surplus - prioritisation needed."
6
CFP Sheet Calculations

The CFP (Certified Financial Planner) Reference Sheet is an internal-only page in the report. It contains pre-computed numbers so the advisor walks into the meeting fully prepared.

6.1 Cash Flow Statement

Monthly Cash Flow Monthly Income = Gross Annual Income / 12
Surplus = Income - Living Expenses - EMIs

6.2 Emergency Fund Gap

Formula EF Target = Monthly Expenses × 6
Gap = EF Target - Liquid Assets

6.3 Life Insurance Adequacy (HLV Method)

Human Life Value HLV Target = (Gross Annual Income × 10) + Outstanding Liabilities
Life Gap = HLV Target - Existing Term Cover

6.4 Health Insurance

Benchmark Calculation Health Target = Rs. 5,00,000 + Rs. 3,00,000 × max(0, Family Size - 1)

6.5 Debt Clearance Priority (Avalanche Method)

All liabilities are sorted by interest rate (highest first). The advisor is told to target the highest-rate debt first — this is mathematically optimal and saves the most interest.

6.6 Standard Assumptions

Education Inflation10% p.a.Education costs rise faster than CPI
Pre-Retirement Equity Return10% p.a.Long-term Nifty 50 CAGR
Post-Retirement Real Return5% p.a.Conservative balanced portfolio

Advisor Intelligence Layer

7.1 Advisor Brief

An auto-generated paragraph summarising the client profile in plain English: age, occupation, family situation, top goal, income level, key strengths, and the most critical red flags. Written in the second person ("Your client is...") so the advisor can scan it in 30 seconds before the meeting.

7.2 Dynamic Questions to Raise

The system generates 5-8 specific probing questions for the advisor, based on which flags are RED or YELLOW:

Trigger FlagGenerated Question
Life Cover"What is your current term cover? Any group life cover from employer?"
Liquidity"Are your liquid assets accessible within 48 hours, or are most FDs locked?"
Health Cover"Is the employer cover portable if you change jobs? Considered a super top-up?"
Credit Card"Is the revolving balance on one card or multiple? What is the interest rate?"
RE Concentration"Is the property self-occupied or an investment? Any intention to liquidate?"
Goals Tension"How would you prioritise between education and retirement if you had to choose?"
Q15 mentions ULIP"Given your ULIP experience, are you open to term insurance specifically?"

7.3 Observations (Areas Needing Attention)

Each RED or YELLOW metric generates a plain-English observation sentence. These are displayed with colour-coded alert bars in the report so the advisor's eye is immediately drawn to the most critical issues.

Design Principles

  • Zero mojibake: All special characters (bullets, dashes, rupee signs) are sanitised through cleanPdfText() before rendering
  • Embedded fonts: Noto Sans Regular + Bold are Base64-encoded into the build to avoid runtime font fetching
  • Page numbers: "Page X of Y" on every page via render={({pageNumber, totalPages})}
  • No raw scores: The Q1-Q14 score table is deliberately excluded — raw scores without question text are meaningless to advisors
The Goal: An advisor should be able to pick up this report 5 minutes before a meeting and walk in feeling like they've known the client for months. Every number is actionable, every flag points to a conversation, and every calculation is pre-done so the advisor can focus on advising.
